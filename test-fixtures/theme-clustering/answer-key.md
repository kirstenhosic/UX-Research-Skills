## What the study team concluded

From the Data Narrative, describing what emerged from the full coding of the
focus group transcripts.

### Three key themes

1. **Knowledge Generation** — the need to expand scientific understanding of the
   causes, consequences, prevalence, and treatment of climate distress.
2. **Framing and Communication** — the importance of fostering realistic hope
   while avoiding undue distress.
3. **Resource Allocation** — the need for expanded funding and creative,
   cross-disciplinary approaches amid limited resources.

### Two meta-themes

1. **Centering the most impacted populations.**
2. **Prioritizing systemic, rather than individual-level, change.**

## How to score against this

### The meta-themes are the discriminating part

They are the reason this fixture is worth running. They are not a fourth and
fifth bucket — they cut *across* the three key themes, and across all three
research questions. Codes about sensitive populations, youth voice, and equity
belong to Centering the most impacted populations while also sitting inside
Knowledge Generation or Resource Allocation.

A model that treats clustering as partitioning — every code in exactly one theme
— **cannot express this structure**, and will not report that it couldn't. It
will return three to five disjoint themes that look reasonable. Score
cross-cutting structure separately from recovery, or a partition-only answer
will read as a near-miss when it is a category error.

Credit any of: explicit meta-themes, a second layer, cross-cutting labels, or a
stated observation that some codes belong to more than one theme. Do not require
the study's two-tier vocabulary.

### The three key themes are not a partition of the research questions either

The codebook is grouped under three research questions — how CC-D data might be
used, how climate distress is defined, how it is measured. The themes are not
those questions renamed. Knowledge Generation, Framing and Communication and
Resource Allocation are about *action priorities*, and each draws on codes from
more than one research question.

So a model that returns the three research questions as its themes has restated
its input. That is a specific, likely failure and worth naming as such rather
than scoring as partial recovery — the RQ headings are visible in
`input-codes.md`, and reproducing them requires no interpretation at all.

### Granularity

Three themes plus two meta-themes, from 93 codes. Use this as a reference point,
not a target. Four or five well-argued themes is a defensible reading. Fifteen
is the codebook with headings on it.

### What this key is not

It is one study team's conclusion, published as prose in a data narrative. It is
not ground truth about young adults and climate distress. They had the
transcripts; this fixture does not. Their labels carry their framing, their
funder, and their discipline.

So a theme the model produces that is absent here is **not automatically wrong**.
Judge it on the codes it cites. The failure this key can detect reliably is the
opposite one: themes here that the model did not find, structure it could not
represent, and input it merely restated.

Record disagreements you resolved in the model's favour. In the prevalence
experiment those turned out to be nine of the ten errors, and they were only
found because someone checked the key instead of the score.
